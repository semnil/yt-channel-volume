# Loudness decisions

> 日本語版: [loudness.ja.md](loudness.ja.md)

Closed decisions about the loudness the extension shows for live streams and their archives, and about how Auto follows a stream on air. The rules in force are in `CLAUDE.md`, "The level of the rendition the player plays" and "A live stream's own level". The decisions about which rendition's level is taken, and what a stored gain is held against, are in [rendition-loudness.md](rendition-loudness.md).

## A loudness shown for a live archive is the value YouTube computed

- **Decision** (2026-10-24): a report said that a loudness had begun to appear for live streams and archives whose processing looked unfinished. It was closed with nothing changed. The level the extension shows there is one YouTube has computed for the audio, not a provisional or a stray value, and showing it is not a defect.
- **Reason**: the extension takes a level from the player's volume line only where the line names `cont.`. A stream on air names no `cont.`, so the saved gain plays there. The decision rests on the player naming `cont.` only for audio whose loudness YouTube has computed, which the response then carries as `loudnessDb`, or as the `perceptualLoudnessDb` and `loudnessTargetLkfs` that stand in for it (`CLAUDE.md`, "Key design decisions").
- **Rejected**: stopping the `playing` answer, or the Loudness in the popup, wherever `isLiveContent` is true. A processed archive is `isLiveContent=true` as well, so this would take away its Loudness, and Auto's gain and save with it, where they are right.
- **Reopen when**: a state is found in which the volume line names `cont.` while the response carries neither `loudnessDb` nor the `perceptualLoudnessDb` and `loudnessTargetLkfs` that stand in for it. That state, and not `isLiveContent`, is what a change would have to be keyed on. Before a loudness on a live stream or an archive is taken as a defect, look for that state first: the popup-open diagnostics (`CLAUDE.md`, "Development notes") carry the volume line (`playerVolume`) and each response's `loudnessDb`; `perceptualLoudnessDb` and `loudnessTargetLkfs` are read in the page's console, as the same note says.

## How the live follow came to its present form

- **Decision** (2026-11-01): with Auto on, a stream on air is asked for its level every second, and the gain played is the one the level asks for, up or down at once ([#128](https://github.com/semnil/yt-channel-volume/pull/128)). That nothing slows Auto's follow is decided for every extension that takes the shared files, and is recorded in [channel-volume-core](https://github.com/semnil/channel-volume-core), `docs/decisions/auto.md`.
- **History**: the follow went through three earlier forms, each described in the pull request that brought it. [#71](https://github.com/semnil/yt-channel-volume/pull/71) read and followed the live level every 30 s. [#73](https://github.com/semnil/yt-channel-volume/pull/73) added a start gate, which held back a first level whose gain would be 10 dB or more over the stored one. [#78](https://github.com/semnil/yt-channel-volume/pull/78) read every 10 s, raised a higher gain slowly, replaced the gate with a start cap and a rise cut, and stored nothing while one of them held the gain. #128 took out the slow raise, the start cap and the rise cut. Two decisions made on the way were replaced by #128. On 2026-10-27 the stretch right after a stream starts, in which the stored gain plays until the player names a live level and the follow picks it up, was accepted as designed, and a bridge that kept watching for the level was not taken; since #128 the level is asked for every second. On 2026-10-30 the start cap, the rise cut and storing nothing while they hold were closed without being checked on a stream on air; #128 took all three out.
